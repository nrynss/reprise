## What was built

The mix runs the user stem through `afftdn` at 25 dB after the resample
and before the sum. It then trims 1200 samples, which is the delay that
filter adds at 48 kHz. The host stem is not filtered. The stored stem
bytes are not rewritten. `HashInputs` includes the denoise amount, so a
tuning change rerenders.

## Measurement

An impulse at sample 4800 peaks at sample 6000 after `afftdn=nr=25`.
The delay is 1200 samples. The resample stage alone adds none. ffmpeg
here is 9.0.2.

The fixture is 12 seconds of a gated 440 Hz tone over seeded white
noise. `astats` reads the written wavs, not the mix log.

The pause floor moves from -55.73 dB to -80.56 dB. That is a drop of
24.82 dB. The speech band moves from -46.85 dB to -46.94 dB. That is
0.09 dB.

The before file is the same mix without the denoiser. Folding mono to
stereo already lowers each channel by about 3 dB, so comparing the raw
stem to one mix channel hides the speech result.

## What surprised us

The first comparison used the mono stem against one channel of the
stereo mix. Speech looked 3.11 dB quieter. Almost all of that is the
existing mono-to-stereo fold, not the denoiser. The denoiser itself
moves the speech band by 0.09 dB.

Keel v0.3.0 exports no denoise. `keel/edl` normalises loudness and
`keel/ffmpeg` only runs the binary. This chain sits on the guest stem
only, before loudness, and trims a delay so this episode clock holds.
A second consumer would not take that unchanged. It stays in the render
package. No library issue.
