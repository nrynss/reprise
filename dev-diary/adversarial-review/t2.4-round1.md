## Findings

### H-1: ending before the socket opens orphans a billed session and hangs

Severity: H.
Where: `web/src/lib/voice/socket.ts:65` (open handler) and `web/src/lib/voice/socket.ts:105` (end).

What: `end()` on a socket that has not opened sets the latch and sends
nothing. When the socket opens later, the open handler still sends
`session.update`. That starts a provider session after the user already
ended the take. `session.end` never follows, because the latch is set.
The `end()` promise never settles, so `endTake` waits forever on the
ending screen. A probe measured both symptoms: after `end()` then
`open()`, the sent log holds only `session.update` and the waiter stays
pending. The orphaned connection bills connected seconds. The token cap
does not stop billing on its own. The mock path can never trigger this,
because the mock opens synchronously. The demo survives it.

Pin: a unit test with a fake handle. Call `end()` before `open()`.
Race the returned promise against a 500 ms timeout and expect it to
resolve. Then call `open()` and expect the sent types to carry no
`session.update` after the end. Both assertions fail on this hash. The
first pends. The second sees `session.update` with no `session.end`.

Mutation: in the open handler, send `session.update` without checking
the end latch. In `end()`, send nothing and resolve nothing on the
unopened path. Each one liner restores one symptom. Together they
restore the defect exactly.
